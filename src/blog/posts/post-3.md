# Drowning in Information, Starving for Wisdom

With the current situation happening, I wonder what it does to a person to be fed this much information, this fast, with no time to process any of it?

I was watching a video by uncivilized on *“Why the Epstein Files Didn't Start a Revolution”,* and that is when I realised I myself, like the speaker, have less sensitivity to the topics of the mass-killing happening in Gaza, or the war in Iran, or the people contained due to ICE. I do pray things get better, but it has become everyday that something new is going wrong, uncontrolled by the public, where the leadership fucks everything and the masses are left to suffer.

[![Why the Epstein Files Didn't Start a Revolution](https://img.youtube.com/vi/qQU8HBzKpTU/maxresdefault.jpg)](https://youtu.be/qQU8HBzKpTU)


I can probably trace it back to late 2019 during the spread of COVID-19, where, due to a lack of conducting comprehensive investigations, the reason for the occurrence of a pandemic wasn't ruled out, and now the world remains vulnerable. But why, when together we could investigate and put an end, we’d rather point a finger to blame.

Then came the Trump administration, which set off a chain of events: an alleged rigged voting system, abortion made illegal, ICE detaining and, in some cases, killing citizens "legally," and then the release of the Epstein files.

With enough pressure from the public, officials released 3.5 million redacted pages out of an estimated 20–40 TB of data, including roughly 180,000 images and 2,000 videos. Enough witnesses were still alive, yet the government relied on the notes of a dead man as proof. Many have been implicated in these crimes, but none have been arrested. A country led by a criminal, and come to think of it, the USA might not be the only one.

Soon after this news reached the public, genocide continued in Palestine and continues still, even after extensive humanitarian efforts. People remain trapped in their homeland while the U.S. partners with Israel to extend its territory. Confessions from soldiers who enjoy killing, children trapped and shot dead, all suffering, all raising their voices to a government that offers no answer, holds no one accountable. Only death and lies remain.

Now genocide continues elsewhere too like Darfur, the bombing of Iran and once again with no one held to account. Wars continue: the Russo-Ukrainian war among them. I find myself wondering: who decides which people are no longer needed? What is the basis for these killings? Is mortality simply a scale for profit? Is *any* of this still just happening for profit?

And then there are exam paper leaks, like NEET, through which students have lost hope. The education ministers and political leaders who oversee this don't send their own children to study in the country they're building; what hope does that leave the common person? Corruption, even when caught, goes unpunished. Rape is a daily occurrence, yet instead of building stronger laws to prevent it, the government would rather rewrite the income tax code. So who, exactly, is doing the work that matters? Even Nepal overthrew its government, yet in India, we remain divided, desensitized, focused on personal gain.

The public keeps raising its voice. Non-violent protests continue across the globe. Yet the people elected to serve us refuse even to have a conversation.

If so much has been hidden for so long, why is it surfacing now? Why increase the public's distrust in government? Why train the media to repeat the same lines, word-for-word, across every channel? Who benefits from this?

I'm not aware of the answers. But I know I cannot let go of my humanity. "Do not accept violence, resist it". But what do we do when the other side *is* violent?

I don't have the answers. I don't know why now, why all at once, why the distrust keeps deepening instead of being addressed. What I know is that I don't want to become numb, and I don't want to become violent either, even when violence starts to look like the only language power listens to. Maybe that's the real question of this moment: not who is right — Luigi, Nepal, or the protester in Delhi standing quietly with a sign — but whether we can hold on to our humanity while the people meant to answer for all of this simply refuse to speak.

The world is drowning in information. What it's starving for is wisdom. And wisdom takes time, the one thing we're never given anymore.
